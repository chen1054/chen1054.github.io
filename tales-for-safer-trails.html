Introduction

The Challenge

Substance use health carries heavy social stigma, creating fear and uncertainty that prevent individuals from seeking help early. Parents often feel unprepared or worry that bringing up these topics too soon might spark unwanted curiosity in young children.

Dual-Persona Framing

Sarah, 28, Nurse & Mom

Needs simple, quick "scripts" and non-judgmental guidance to educate her kids without adding stress to her busy daily routine.

Emma, 7, Grade 2

Needs clear, relatable rules and a safe space to ask questions without fear or confusion caused by complex medical terms.

"How might we normalize substance use health conversations as a routine part of daily parenting for children under 12 years old?"

Key Insights

Bridging the Gap via Play

Traditional warnings alienate children; anthropomorphic characters (personified objects) transform abstract risks into engaging, non-threatening stories.

Institutional Trust

Public health materials must align with provincial educational standards to earn trust from parents, schools, and health professionals.

Core Solution & Strategic Alignment

Scalable Story World

To solve the engagement gap, I conceived and designed the Scalable Story World

A progressive narrative framework

Bridges parent-child communication across different child developmental stage.

Persistent Character Arc

Features Milo and Mia growing up alongside Patchy the Guardian (a superhero band-aid), giving children familiar companions throughout their early childhood.

Progressive Complexity Engine

As the story world scales, language, themes, and interactive difficulty evolve across three age tiers

To reflect this evolving framework, the project was titled “Tales for Safer Trails.” The logo features a playful, winding dashed line connecting “TALES FOR” and “SAFER TRAILS”—visually representing a trail that symbolizes a child’s journey of growth, exploration, and learning to navigate the world safely.

The Core Product: Double-Sided One-Pager System

Front Side (Parent Story): Guided storytelling scripts for parents to facilitate open conversations.

Back Side (Child Activity): Interactive puzzles, coloring, and games to reinforce safety concepts through play.

All modules strictly align with the Ontario HPE Curriculum (2019) and Ottawa Public Health Prevention Guidelines.

Main Characters- Milo & Mia

Guardian - Patchy

Ages 4-5 - The "Ask First" Rule

When Milo and Mia find a bottle and their curiosity leads them to open the medicine bottle, Patchy the Guardian appears to help them understand the situation.*

* Ontario HPE Curriculum (2019):

•Page 81 (Safety and Well-Being 24.1): "Demonstrate an understanding of essential safety procedures and why they are important."

•Page 84 (Self-Regulation 2.2): "Demonstrate an ability to use a decision-making process to assess risks and make safe choices.”

OPH Substance Use and Harm Prevention:

•"Encouraging children to identify and rely on a 'Safe Helper' before interacting with unknown items."

•"Teaching basic refusal skills and effective communication strategies for children when faced with curiosity or peer influence involving unknown substances."

Ages 6-8 - Mighty Brains

When Milo and Mia see their mother drinking a glass of wine, they become curious about the beverage and why adults can have it while children cannot. Their mother teaches them about the effects of alcohol on the body and brain.*

* Ontario HPE Curriculum (2019):

•Page 161 (Grade 2 - C1.2 Substance Use): "Demonstrate an understanding of how drugs and other substances can affect the way the body works

•Page 181 (Grade 3 - C1.1 Substances): "Describe the effects of different types of legal and illegal substances... on the mind and body."

•Page 139 (Grade 1 - C3.2 Decision-Making): "Explain how to apply a decision-making process to make safe choices regarding their own health."

OPH Substance Use and Harm Prevention:

•"Encouraging open, age-appropriate dialogue between parents and children to build a foundation of trust regarding substance use before the onset of peer pressure."

•"Educating youth on the vulnerability of the developing brain to psychoactive substances to delay the age of first use."

Ages 9-12 - Don't be fooled!

Milo and Mia meet with their friend Sam when they see him taking a hit from his vape. Sam tells them it's harmless and they should try it too, but the kids think that something is not quite right. *

Ontario HPE Curriculum (2019):

•Page 156 (C3.1 Grade 4): "Identify key nutrients... and substances that can affect health (vaping, tobacco)."

•Page 173 (C1.1 Grade 5): "Identify factors that could affect a person’s decision to use or not use substances (peer pressure, media influence)."

OPH Substance Use and Harm Prevention:

•"Empowering pre-teens to critically analyze social and media influences, specifically focusing on debunking myths surrounding vaping and nicotine."

OPH School Health Online (Vaping Prevention):

•"Providing evidence-based information on the long-term health consequences of vaping on the developing adolescent brain and respiratory system."

Digital Touchpoint: OPH Resource Hub & Online Tool

To make these materials accessible across Ottawa without economic barriers, we designed a digital experience integrated into the OPH ecosystem:

OPH Dedicated Sub-site (Web UX/UI)

A responsive web resource center where parents can easily filter, preview, and download age-appropriate One-Pagers.

In-Browser Doodle Editor (Web Tool PoC)

Built a React/TypeScript drawing widget directly on the site. Households without a physical printer can color, complete activities, and save completed One-Pagers digitally on their devices.

Launch Doodle Demo

Multi-Channel Campaign & Impact

We deployed a integrated offline and online strategy to drive traffic to the digital hub:

Offline Channels:

Community Posters: QR-enabled posters displayed in public spaces (libraries, schools, community centers) for direct scanning.

School Distribution: Distributed printable One-Pagers directly through local schools for students to complete with parents at home.

Online Channels:

Social Media Campaigns: Targeted Instagram and Facebook ad units running on official OPH channels.

Potential for Impact

Behavioral Normalization

Transformed a historically taboo public health subject into an approachable, routine parenting habit through an ongoing, progressive narrative world.

Scalable Community Reach

Bridged the communication gap between classrooms and households via an omni-channel ecosystem—connecting digital discovery with physical, in-backpack school distribution.

Long-term Health Literacy

Scaffolded age-appropriate health education across ages 4 to 12, building a preventative foundation of trust and decision-making skills well before children encounter adolescent peer pressures.

Sustained Digital Growth

Positioned the One-Pager tool as an organic entry point, steadily funneling families into Ottawa Public Health’s broader support networks and institutional resources.

"Ultimately, our solution moves beyond static educational collateral—it provides parents with a sustainable, adaptive framework that evolves alongside their children’s development."
