Overview & Strategic Context

The Inspiration

Every household shares a familiar, everyday frustration: standing in front of an open refrigerator at the end of a busy day, wondering,

"What should I cook tonight?"

While cooking at home is meant to be nourishing, the daily chore of meal planning often turns into exhaustion. Fresh groceries are forgotten and left to spoil, grocery bills climb, and families find themselves stuck in a repetitive loop of takeout or monotonous meals. We created MenuMe to transform everyday cooking from a daily source of stress into an effortless, budget-friendly ritual.

The Problem & Solution

Without intuitive, structured support, home cooks constantly juggle two opposing pressures:

Mental Overload

Daily decision fatigue leads to repetitive, nutritionally imbalanced meals.

Household Waste & Rising Costs

Disconnected grocery planning causes unused ingredients to go bad, driving up both environmental impact and grocery expenses.

A Dual-Loop Culinary Framework

Fridge-to-Recipe (Waste Prevention)

Scans and inventories existing ingredients to instantly suggest practical, healthy recipes, cutting food waste right at the source.

Specials-to-Pantry (Budget Optimization)

Aggregates local grocery store promotions, turning weekly discounts into curated meal plans and smart shopping lists ready for pickup or delivery.

Research & Audience Insights

Focus Group Key Findings

Our team conducted user focus groups to validate assumptions and gather qualitative requirements:

1

100% of participants reported standing paralyzed before open refrigerator doors and expressed deep frustration over throwing away spoiled produce and bakery items.

2

Users emphasized cost management and requested direct integrations with supermarket promotions and one-click delivery services.

3

Participants rejected invasive pop-ups, spam communications, and tedious onboarding, demanding meal recommendations achievable within five minutes.

Information Architecture & Core User Flows

App Sitemap & Structural Hierarchy

To establish a clear mental model before wireframing, we architected the system navigation around four core functional pillars: Menu Planner, Fridge Inventory, Recipe Sharing, and Profile & Settings. This structure ensures that users can toggle seamlessly between daily cooking decisions and weekly grocery logistics.

Core Task Flows (Logic & Decision Frameworks)

We designed three primary user flows to validate end-to-end task completion from initial discovery to kitchen execution:

Personalized Menu by Preferences

Guides users through structured preference inputs (household size, dining frequency, cuisine tastes, and dietary limits) to generate customized weekly meal plans.

Weekly Specials to Doorstep

Connects local supermarket discount items with matching recipes, automatically generating curated shopping lists ready for in-store pickup or home delivery.

What’s in My Fridge

Allows users to log or scan on-hand ingredients to dynamically produce viable recipes, supported by an integrated cooking mode.

Usability Testing & Iterative Design

We conducted unmoderated usability tests on Maze across 5 participants to identify navigation bottlenecks and refine the interface using quantitative metrics and qualitative feedback. The findings directly drove our transition from low-fidelity wireframes into polished high-fidelity interfaces:

Personalized Menu Flow: Expanding Preference Customization

Usability Finding: The task registered a 40% drop-off rate and a 41.6% misclick rate. Participants noted that broad, binary lifestyle presets failed to accommodate real-world dietary restrictions.

Design Iteration: In the high-fidelity UI, we broke the single preference screen into granular, multi-select categories covering Allergies, Dislikes, Prep Time Constraints (<15 mins), and Global Cuisines.

Weekly Specials Flow: Balancing Information Density

Usability Finding: A 33.3% drop-off rate highlighted cognitive overload caused by repetitive cards and ambiguous visual hierarchies.

Design Iteration: We enlarged recipe cards, added breathing room, and introduced high-contrast metadata badges (Top Rated, Quick Meal, Budget-Friendly) paired with immediate nutritional parameters (15 min / 380 kcal).

Design System & Cross-Device UI Showcase

Visual Strategy & Design System Tokens

Inspired by our moodboard concept—"Your Menu, Your Rules"—the visual identity balances vibrant culinary energy with calm, organic wellness:

Bright Gold (#F89E3F): Stimulates appetite and optimism, serving as the primary accent for CTAs (Generate Recipes, Order) and active selection states.

Loch Blue (#60B5BF): Represents fresh ingredients, balance, and dietary well-being, grounding the interface with the feel of a trustworthy utility.

Grounded Grey (#E5E5E5): Provides a clean, accessible foundation that keeps rich food photography and nutritional metrics legible and clutter-free.

Component Library: Standardized rounded pill selectors, steppers (+ / -), segmented controls, and minimalist iconography.

End-to-End User Experience Delivery

The Waste-Free Fridge Loop: Users photograph or select remaining groceries, browse instant recipe matches, and enter an unobstructed, step-by-step full-screen cooking mode tailored for busy kitchen environments.

The Weekly Specials Fulfillment Loop: Bridges digital planning with real-world logistics through grocery delivery scheduling, store pickup toggles, and integrated payment flows.

Cross-Platform Responsiveness (Mobile & Tablet Ecosystem)

iOS Mobile: Optimized for one-handed operation on the go, utilizing bottom tab bars, stacked card views, and thumb-friendly shopping list toggles.

iPadOS Tablet: Utilizes a persistent split-screen sidebar layout designed for countertop use while prepping meals, allowing users to view ingredients alongside step-by-step instructions simultaneously.

Key Learnings & Takeaways

Flow Architecture, Empathy, and the Power of Peer Critique

Mapping user flows pushed me to dissect every decision branch and anticipate user mental models, sharpening both my structural logic and user empathy. It also reinforced that UX design cannot happen in a vacuum—independent drafting only goes so far, and cross-checking edge cases through team critique was essential to uncover blind spots and build a cohesive journey.

Validating Beyond Internal Logic with Usability Data

While team reviews aligned our internal logic, testing our prototypes on Maze demonstrated that even well-debated assumptions must be validated against real user behavior. Analyzing quantitative metrics proved that packing comprehensive features into early wireframes often introduced unnecessary friction. Letting real misclick and drop-off data guide our simplifications ultimately created a far more focused and effective experience.

"Ultimately, MenuMe moves beyond a simple recipe index—it provides everyday home cooks with a sustainable, preference-driven ecosystem that bridges pantry inventory with local affordability to permanently simplify mealtime decisions."
